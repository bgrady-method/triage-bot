2. **The leading-edge signal worked.** C1 (alert-system BSN4EQX43 at 20:14:43Z) fired 8m30s before the human swat report. If a routine were polling every 5 minutes instead of every 60, it could have alerted Ben at ~20:18Z, ~5 minutes before users reported. **Consider a 5-min poll for alert-system specifically** during business hours.
3. **The fe-errors 4-alert "tight repeating cap" hypothesis from the prior cycle was wrong.** A1 had 2 alerts in this burst, A2 had 2 — not 4. The pattern broke at n=3 cycles. Revise the hypothesis: fe-errors B011R3D650X count is NOT a fixed cap; it tracks underlying error volume which today scaled with the incident.
4. **Add a "cross-channel co-firing" detector to the routine.** When ≥3 of the 4 alert channels fire within 30 min, escalate to a unified-incident DM regardless of strict-heuristic group counts. Today's incident produced 10 strict-rule groups; treating them as one would have given Ben one clear DM instead of fragmented per-group output.
5. **Operator-driven thread on swat parent was the BEST signal in the entire poll cycle.** The human reports gave clearer root-cause hints (RabbitMQ, designer slow, sidebar login, Google OAuth) than any monitor would have. Whenever a swat thread has 10+ replies and named subteams paged, the routine should treat it as ground truth.
