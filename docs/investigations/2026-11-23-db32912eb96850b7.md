### Key findings
- In every one of the 21 exceptions, `sync-ops` goes from 33 to 49 and `conn-sec` from 6.0 to 21.5 s. That is a newly created multiplexer in a freshly started process. For about 20 s it had no connection to the Valkey node that owns slot 15206 (172.31.41.252), so sync GET/SETEX/PSETEX calls timed out in the backlog after 5 s.
- The process restart is visible in APM. `classic/syncservices` served 0 requests on both prod-classic nodes for about 70 s (14:14:30–14:15:40Z), and the other classic/* apps on the same hosts were unaffected. That means a syncservices-scoped app or pool restart, not a host or IIS-wide event.
- Only -02 logged Redis errors. On -01 the restart did not produce backlog timeouts.
- There were no 5xx responses on prod. Impact is about 70 s of paused QBDT web-connector sync traffic, which was absorbed by the catch-up burst.
- 24h baseline: 0 classic/syncservices Redis errors in the prior 7 days. The only prior 30-day occurrence was 11-04 14h (6 errors). That is the same hour of day, so it may be the same restart pattern; not verified.

### Likely cause
A `classic/syncservices` app restart on both prod-classic nodes at about 14:14:30Z. The trigger is unknown: a TFS deploy, a pool recycle, or a web.config change. On prod-classic-02, the new process's StackExchange.Redis connection took about 20 s to establish, and the first cache operations timed out in the backlog. This is a transient startup artifact, not a Valkey outage: no other Redis client service had any errors.

## Lessons / follow-up
- In `RedisTimeoutException` messages, a low `sync-ops` with a small `conn-sec` is a reliable "process just started" tell. Check it before treating a Redis-errors fire as a Valkey or server-side problem.
- Optional hardening for Connect: warm the multiplexer (`ConnectionMultiplexer.Connect` plus a PING) in `Application_Start`, or treat cache failures in `RedisCacheManager` as cache misses during startup.
